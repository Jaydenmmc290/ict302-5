## How early teens get phones

Most teens today receive their first smartphone around 10 or 11 (martin et al., 2025). Phones are often seen as tools for safety, staying in touch with family, and keeping up with friends. By the time teens start high school, it is rare not to have one.

## Always connected

According to a 2024 Pew study, nearly all teen are online "almost constantly." Group chats, social media, and apps are part of daily life. Staying connected makes it easy to share news, plan meetups, or just check in. However, this also means there's always something new popping up on your screen trying to steal your attention.

## The pressure to reply

Constant notifications can create real pressure to respond fast, whether from group chats, friends, or social media. Not replying quickly can lead to anxiety about missing out or letting people down. This pressure doesn't disappear when you are behind the wheel, making it tough to ignore your phones even when you know it's unsafe.

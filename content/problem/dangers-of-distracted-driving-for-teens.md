# The Dangers of Distracted Driving for Teenagers

Driving requires attention, awareness, and quick decision-making. For teenage drivers, these responsibilities can be especially challenging because they are still gaining experience behind the wheel. When a driver looks at a phone, talks to passengers, or focuses on something other than the road, even for a few seconds, the consequences can be serious.

Distracted driving is a major road safety problem. Learning about its dangers can help teenagers make safer decisions and protect themselves, their passengers, and everyone else on the road.

## Why Are Teen Drivers at Risk?

Teenagers are still developing their driving skills and learning how to respond to unexpected situations. Distractions can make it harder to recognize hazards, judge distances, and react quickly.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) reports that drivers ages 15–20 had a higher percentage of distraction in fatal crashes involving distracted drivers than drivers age 21 and older in its 2019 data. The CDC also reported that 39% of high school students who drove had texted or emailed while driving on at least one day during the previous 30 days in a 2019 survey.

These findings show why distracted driving deserves special attention among young drivers.

## Common Distractions for Teenagers

Distractions are not limited to texting. Anything that takes attention away from driving can create danger.

Some common distractions include:

- **Cell phones:** Reading messages, answering calls, checking notifications, or using social media.
- **Passengers:** Talking, joking, arguing, or turning around to interact with friends.
- **Music and entertainment:** Changing songs, adjusting volume, or searching for playlists.
- **Food and drinks:** Eating, drinking, or reaching for items while driving.
- **Navigation systems:** Entering an address or looking at directions while the vehicle is moving.

Even activities that seem harmless can take attention away from the road at the wrong moment.

## The Three Types of Distraction

Distracted driving generally involves three types of distraction:

1. **Visual distraction:** Taking your eyes off the road, such as looking at a text message.
2. **Manual distraction:** Taking your hands off the steering wheel, such as reaching for a phone.
3. **Cognitive distraction:** Taking your mind off driving, such as becoming focused on a conversation.

Texting is particularly dangerous because it can involve all three types at the same time.

According to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), reading or sending a text can take a driver's eyes off the road for about five seconds. At 55 miles per hour, that is enough time to travel approximately the length of a football field without looking at the road.

## The Consequences of Distracted Driving

Distracted driving can lead to serious consequences, including:

- Vehicle crashes and injuries.
- Serious or fatal harm to drivers, passengers, pedestrians, and cyclists.
- Damage to vehicles and property.
- Legal penalties for breaking distracted driving laws.
- Increased insurance costs and other financial consequences.
- Emotional stress and long-term effects on families and communities.

NHTSA reported that 3,208 people were killed and an estimated 315,167 people were injured in traffic crashes involving distracted drivers in 2024.

These numbers show that distracted driving is not just a personal risk. It affects entire communities.

## How Teenagers Can Avoid Distracted Driving

Teen drivers can take simple steps to reduce distractions:

1. Put your phone away before starting the vehicle.
2. Turn on Do Not Disturb or a driving-focus setting.
3. Set your GPS and music before leaving.
4. Avoid eating or handling other items while driving.
5. Ask passengers to help you stay focused instead of distracting you.
6. Never read or respond to messages while driving.
7. If you need to use your phone, find a safe place to pull over and park first.

Making these habits part of every trip can help prevent dangerous situations.

## Take Responsibility Behind the Wheel

Getting a driver's license is an important milestone, but it also comes with responsibility. Teenagers should understand that driving requires their full attention. A message, notification, or conversation can wait. Arriving safely is more important than responding immediately.

Every driver can help make roads safer by putting distractions aside and focusing on driving.

**Remember: Eyes on the road, hands on the wheel, and mind on driving.**

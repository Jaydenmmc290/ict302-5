## Simulator results

A 2022 driving simulator study (Jeihani et al.) found that when phones were fully blocked during driving, participants performed almost as safely as when they had no distractions at all. This suggests that strong phone locks can make a noticeable difference in reducing distracted driving risks.

## Why people don't turn blockers on

Despite their benefits, only about 23% of people in the study had ever used a driving blocker. Many said blockers were hard to set up, easy to disable, or not built into their phones by default. Over half of participants said they'd be more likely to use phone safety features if they were automatic or built into the operating system.

## The role of parent feedback

Parent feedback from past studies (Creaser, 2015) highlights how families want safety tools that are easy to manage, transparent, and difficult to bypass. Built-in features that work automatically give both parents and teens more peace of mind about safety on the road.

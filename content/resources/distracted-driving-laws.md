Distracted driving is not only dangerous; it can also be illegal. Across the United States, laws have been created to discourage drivers from texting, using handheld phones, and engaging in activities that take their attention away from the road.

These laws are especially important for young drivers, who are still developing safe driving habits.

## Distracted Driving Laws in the United States

Distracted driving laws vary from state to state. Many states prohibit texting while driving, while others have additional restrictions on handheld cell phone use.

According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), 49 states and Washington, D.C., prohibit texting while driving for all drivers.

Some states also have stricter rules for young drivers, including restrictions on using phones while driving.

You can check the laws in individual states through the [Governors Highway Safety Association](https://www.ghsa.org/state-laws-issues/distracted-driving).

## Kentucky Distracted Driving Laws

Kentucky has specific laws that restrict the use of communication devices while driving.

Under Kentucky Revised Statute (KRS) 189.292, drivers are prohibited from manually writing, sending, or reading text-based communications while operating a moving motor vehicle on a roadway.

The law includes certain exceptions, such as:

- Using a GPS feature on a personal communication device.
- Using a navigation system integrated into the vehicle.
- Selecting or entering a phone number to make a call.
- Sending an emergency message to request medical help or contact public safety authorities.

You can read the complete law on the [Kentucky Legislature website](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/Statutes/statute.aspx?id=39750).

## Special Rules for Teen Drivers

Kentucky has additional restrictions for drivers under 18 years old.

Under KRS 189.294, drivers under 18 who hold an instruction permit, intermediate license, or operator's license cannot use a personal communication device while operating a moving motor vehicle, motorcycle, or moped on a roadway.

The main exception is using a device to request medical help or contact law enforcement or public safety authorities during an emergency.

This means teenage drivers must be especially careful about phone use, even when they are not actively texting.

Read the full law here:

[Kentucky Revised Statute 189.294](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/Statutes/statute.aspx?id=39751).

## Why These Laws Matter

Distracted driving laws exist to protect drivers, passengers, pedestrians, and everyone else sharing the road.

However, laws alone cannot prevent every distracted-driving incident. Drivers must also make responsible decisions and develop safer habits.

The CDC explains that enforcement efforts and public awareness campaigns can help reduce handheld phone use while driving.

## How Drivers Can Stay Within the Law

Here are some practical ways to avoid distracted driving:

1. Put your phone away before starting the vehicle.
2. Set your GPS and music before driving.
3. Avoid reading or responding to messages while driving.
4. Use a passenger to handle calls or messages when necessary.
5. If you need to use your phone, pull over to a safe location and park first.
6. Follow the distracted-driving laws in the state where you are driving.
